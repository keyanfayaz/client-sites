# Playbooks

Written for the person having a bad day. Each one assumes you are under time pressure and have not read it before.

---

## 🚨 Line Down

**Trigger:** production has stopped and cannot restart within fifteen minutes.

<div class="bg-white p-6 rounded-lg shadow-md border-l-4 border-red-500 my-6">
  <ol class="text-gray-700 space-y-3 text-sm">
    <li><strong>Make it safe.</strong> Isolate the machine and apply lockout before any inspection.</li>
    <li><strong>Call it out loud.</strong> Tell your supervisor. Do not begin diagnosis before the call is made.</li>
    <li><strong>Raise the ticket.</strong> Line-down priority in the maintenance system. Include cell, machine, and what you observed — not what you think caused it.</li>
    <li><strong>Escalate on the clock.</strong> Thirty minutes to the shift lead, sixty to the plant manager, ninety to the VP of Operations. The clock does not pause for diagnosis.</li>
    <li><strong>Notify Planning.</strong> They decide what reroutes and which customer gets the call.</li>
    <li><strong>Write it up before the end of shift.</strong> While you still remember the sequence.</li>
  </ol>
</div>

**Do not** attempt a repair outside your certification, and do not defeat an interlock to keep running. Both are dismissal matters.

## 🔍 Nonconformance

**Trigger:** a part does not meet the drawing, at any stage.

1. **Quarantine it.** Red tag, move to the hold area. A nonconforming part that stays in flow will end up shipped.
2. **Do not rework yet.** Rework before disposition destroys the evidence and the traceability.
3. **Raise an NCR** in the quality system with the part number, heat lot, operation, and measured deviation.
4. **Quality dispositions it** — use as is, rework, or scrap. Only Quality decides, never Production.
5. **If it already shipped**, tell your account engineer immediately. Customer notification is a contractual obligation with a clock on it.

<div class="bg-amber-50 border-l-4 border-amber-500 p-5 rounded-r-lg my-6">
  <p class="text-sm text-amber-800"><strong>The rule that matters:</strong> nobody has ever been disciplined here for raising an NCR. People have been dismissed for hiding one.</p>
</div>

## 📞 Customer Complaint

| Step | Who | Timing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acknowledge receipt | Account engineer | Within 4 working hours |
| Contain — stop further shipment | Quality | Same day |
| Root cause investigation | Quality plus owning area | Within 5 working days |
| Corrective action plan issued | Quality | Within 10 working days |
| Effectiveness review | Quality | At 90 days |

Never speculate about cause to a customer before the investigation concludes. "We are investigating and will come back to you by Thursday" is always the right answer.

## 🚚 Emergency Dispatch

**Trigger:** a Full Program customer is line-down and needs stock today.

1. Confirm entitlement — emergency dispatch is contractual, not universal
2. Check stock across all three warehouses before declining
3. Release under the emergency authority code; normal pick sequencing is bypassed
4. Book the courier directly rather than waiting for the next scheduled run
5. Tell the account engineer so the customer hears it from us first

Cost is not a reason to delay an entitled emergency dispatch. Reconciliation happens afterwards.

---

## 🔄 Changeover

Standard changeover between part families on the same cell:

1. Complete and record the last piece of the outgoing run
2. Clear all outgoing material from the cell — mixed parts is the most common cause of nonconformance here
3. Load the new program and verify the revision against the router
4. Run first article and hold for inspection
5. **Do not begin the production run until first article is signed off**
6. Record changeover time in the scheduler
